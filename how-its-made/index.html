    00 · The view

    9.2km²

    of San Francisco, laser-scanned and rebuilt

    This is the window at golden hour. Nothing out there is a photograph: it is a model of the real city, made from public data and rendered in Blender. Here it is, taken apart.

    The area the 2023 USGS lidar was read for: the view from the window, out to 3.6 km

    01 · Raw shapes

    11,593

    buildings, as plain grey clay

    The ground and every building, from OpenStreetMap's footprints, raised to the heights the laser scan measured. No detail, no colour, no air. Of the trees, only the park's planting below the window, so its beds and paths read.

    • 17,536 footprints in the area
    • 7,818 roofs shaped by the scan
    • 6,028 left out, behind the room

    Footprints © OpenStreetMap contributors; heights from the 2023 USGS lidar

    02 · The detail

    19,564

    real windows, cut into the near walls

    Within 360 m of the window the walls get openings, sills and blinds; the roofs get vents and hatches out to 1.6 km.

    • 1,867 walls
    • 728 bay windows
    • 494 window boxes
    • 458 downpipes
    • 128 AC units
    • 14 fire escapes
    • 113 roofs in full
    • 1,720 roof kits

    On the site since 18 October (the first build had 20,043 windows; a fix for the night re-fitted the storeys)

    03 · X-ray

    2.97billion

    triangles in the golden scene, every copy counted

    Every surface coloured by what it is, with its triangles' edges drawn in. Blue: the plain buildings. Grey: the new detail. Tan: the scanned ground. Red-brown: the courts' deck. The trees come out dark: their edges are that dense.

    • 2.95 bn of them in trees
    • 17.8 M stored once, then copied
    • 789,622 faces of new detail

    Counted inside Blender, in the golden-hour scene

    04 · Trees

    107,737

    trees, each where a real one stands

    Positions from the 2023 laser scan and the city's street tree list. The 2,807 nearest use 16 detailed models.

    • 64 tree models in all
    • 39,559 found in the scan

    Positions from the 2023 USGS lidar and DataSF's Street Tree List

    05 · Materials

    59

    materials on the city

    Colour and texture for walls, roofs, streets, water and trees, before the air goes in. Most of it is made by code, not photographs. The street life arrives here too.

    • 144 image textures
    • 17,492 parked cars
    • 7,425 street lamps
    • 687 moored boats

    Counted in the scene itself

    06 · Light

    moments of one day, each rendered on its own

    07 · Night

    08 · What moves

    15.1

    big ships a day across the Golden Gate

    The view runs on real timetables: ships, the Sausalito ferry, tour boats, sailboats, bridge traffic and planes. Every visitor sees the same ship at the same second.

    • 89 cruise calls in 2026
    • 14 ferry trips a weekday
    • ~95,000 cars a weekday
    • 8 flight corridors

      The data

      Five public sources, one place.

      Laid on top of each other, as the build reads them. The desk is the warm dot; the pale wedge is what the window sees.

      1 · The ground

      1,406,920

      quads of terrain, and the bay floor

      Elevation from 205 open terrain tiles (AWS Open Data). Near the window the ground is the laser scan's own: 463,540 triangles.

      Terrain Tiles on AWS Open Data, from USGS and NOAA elevation data

      2 · The laser scan

      747M

      laser points, streamed and gridded

      USGS lidar flown on 20 April 2023: about 78 returns per square metre, 91 near the window. It gives every roof its height and finds the trees.

      The USGS 3D Elevation Program's 2023 San Francisco survey, gridded at 0.5 m, and 0.25 m near the window

        The build

        Six days, two accounts.

        The first commit was on 13 October at 22:06: "a desk on Russian Hill looking at the Golden Gate". Two Claude accounts have worked on it since, a coordinator and a worker with its helpers, under one rulebook.

        Commits per day

          Rented GPUs: minutes of rendering

            When a job was too big for the Mac, Claude rented graphics cards in the cloud: it started a machine, sent it the scene, rendered, brought the pictures home and shut the machine down again.

            Eight machines over four days, 15 to 18 October, here and there: an hour or two at a time, mornings, afternoons and evenings, once past midnight.

            In all, 9 hours 15 minutes of rendering, about 22 GPU-hours, for 661 renders, on up to four RTX 4090s at once.

            The longest run: 220 renders in two hours on two RTX 5090s. The last: the city at night, 49 renders in 48 minutes on four RTX 4090s.

            Everything else ran on the Mac on the desk, one Blender or one test browser at a time, taking turns: since the morning of 18 October, 203 turns, about 26 hours.
